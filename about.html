Meaning

The lights describe the machine. So what translates them into Street physics — what the regime implies for assets, and what would break the call.

  1. Duration risk — from inflation, rates (yields / dollar / mortgages), and how hot nominal GDP is vs real. Rising = discount rates bite; bonds and long-duration assets have a harder time.
  2. Credit risk — from Growth, the Risk light (vol / spreads / financial conditions), and credit impulse (is private bank lending accelerating?). Falling = cash flows still look collectible; rising = stress on collectability.
  3. Confirm — outputs and cross-checks that agree or quarrel with the causal story (stock–bond correlation, dollar, gold, BTC vs liquidity, and similar).
  4. Falsify — the clean flip that would kill the call (e.g. Inflation turns Cold while Growth stays Strong softens a hard-duration read).

Same 1y · 2y · 5y clock as the lights. Numbers lock first (verified bake); copy follows. Not trade tips — a readable map from regime → duration vs credit → prove me wrong.

Glossary

Percentile
Where today’s level sits in the last 1 / 2 / 5 years. 90th = higher than ~90% of readings in that window.
z-score
How many standard deviations today’s level is from the average over that same window. +1 = one σ above average.
1 · 2 · 5
Years in the comparison window for lights, table, and charts. Default 2.
Lights
Five regime dials (liquidity → rates → growth → inflation → risk). Each opens its shelf. Tap “Tap for who voted” beside the street title for the club card.
Asset (central bank)
Something the bank owns on its balance sheet — usually bonds, sometimes ETFs or loans — not “stable value.”
Reserves
Cash banks hold at the Fed. Fuel for money markets.
TGA
Treasury General Account — the government’s checking account at the Fed. When it fills, cash leaves banks.
ON RRP
Overnight reverse repo — cash parked at the Fed overnight. Rise drains market liquidity.
Net liquidity
Street shorthand: Fed assets − TGA − ON RRP. Rising = more cash in markets.
Real yield
Yield after inflation (TIPS). Higher real yields = tighter for risk assets.
Breakeven
Market’s implied inflation from nominal vs TIPS yields.
OAS
Option-adjusted spread — extra yield a corporate bond pays over Treasuries. Wider = more credit stress.
VIX / MOVE
Implied volatility — equity fear (VIX) and Treasury fear (MOVE).
Curve (2s10s)
Long yield minus short yield. Inverted (negative) often warns of slowdown.
Outputs
Equities, commodities, crypto, gold — read them under Markets; they don’t drive the lights.
Last print
Markets Latest on open (age beside the Markets title). The circle next to the age pulls again. Listed names can lag ~15 minutes. z and percentile stay daily.
Credit impulse
How fast bank credit growth is accelerating or decelerating (change in the YoY rate of loans). Fed plumbing is one liquidity story; private lending is the other.
Nominal − real GDP
Nominal GDP YoY minus real GDP YoY. How much of the expansion is prices vs real activity — core for duration vs credit.
Duration risk
How rate moves hit the present value of future cash flows (bonds, and equity valuations). Rising = harder for long duration.
Credit risk
Uncertainty that cash flows actually arrive (spreads, growth, risk appetite). Rising = markets less sure about collectability.
