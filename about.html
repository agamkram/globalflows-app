How it works

The book is a causal chain. Cash and central-bank plumbing set the tone; rates and the dollar transmit it; growth and inflation say what the real economy is doing; risk appetite prices fear. You read the machine left to right — then ask what that regime implies for assets.

So what

Lights describe the machine. So what translates them into Street physics — duration vs credit, what confirms, what would break the call. Same 1 · 2 · 5 clock. Numbers lock first (verified bake); copy follows.

Duration risk

From inflation, rates (yields / dollar / mortgages), and how hot nominal GDP is vs real. Rising = discount rates bite; bonds and long-duration assets have a harder time.

Credit risk

From Growth, the Risk light (vol / spreads / financial conditions), and credit impulse — is private bank lending accelerating? Falling = cash flows still look collectible.

Confirm

Outputs and cross-checks that agree or quarrel with the causal story — stock–bond correlation, dollar, gold, BTC vs liquidity, and similar.

Falsify

The clean flip that would kill the call — e.g. Inflation turns Cold while Growth stays Strong softens a hard-duration read.

Glossary

Reading the numbers

Percentile
Where today’s level sits in the last 1 / 2 / 5 years. 90th = higher than ~90% of readings in that window.
z-score
How many standard deviations today’s level is from the average over that same window. +1 = one σ above average.
1 · 2 · 5
Years in the comparison window for lights, table, and charts. Default 2.

The instrument

Lights
Five regime dials (liquidity → rates → growth → inflation → risk). Each opens its shelf. Tap “Tap for who voted” for the club card.
Outputs
Equities, commodities, crypto, gold — under Markets; the reacting tape.
Last print
Markets Latest on open (age beside the Markets title). The circle next to the age pulls again. Listed names can lag ~15 minutes. z and percentile stay daily.
So what
Tap today’s regime: what the lights imply for duration and credit, what confirms, and what would falsify the call.
Duration risk
How rate moves hit the present value of future cash flows (bonds, and equity valuations). Rising = harder for long duration.
Credit risk
Uncertainty that cash flows actually arrive (spreads, growth, risk appetite). Rising = markets less sure about collectability.
Credit impulse
How fast bank credit growth is accelerating or decelerating (change in the YoY rate of loans). Fed plumbing is one liquidity story; private lending is the other.
Nominal − real GDP
Nominal GDP YoY minus real GDP YoY. How much of the expansion is prices vs real activity — core for duration vs credit.

Plumbing & rates

Asset (central bank)
Something the bank owns on its balance sheet — usually bonds, sometimes ETFs or loans.
Reserves
Cash banks hold at the Fed. Fuel for money markets.
TGA
Treasury General Account — the government’s checking account at the Fed. When it fills, cash leaves banks.
ON RRP
Overnight reverse repo — cash parked at the Fed overnight. Rise drains market liquidity.
Net liquidity
Street shorthand: Fed assets − TGA − ON RRP. Rising = more cash in markets.
Real yield
Yield after inflation (TIPS). Higher real yields = tighter for risk assets.
Breakeven
Market’s implied inflation from nominal vs TIPS yields.
Curve (2s10s)
Long yield minus short yield. Inverted (negative) often warns of slowdown.

Risk & credit tape

OAS
Option-adjusted spread — extra yield a corporate bond pays over Treasuries. Wider = more credit stress.
VIX / MOVE
Implied volatility — equity fear (VIX) and Treasury fear (MOVE).
