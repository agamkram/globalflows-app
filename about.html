Where the numbers come from

Public sources: FRED, the New York Fed, Yahoo Finance, Bundesbank, Bank of England, Japan’s Ministry of Finance, and S&P earnings yield from multpl. A morning job at 8:47 New York time — after the 8:30 prints, before the open — pulls the book, rebuilds the archive from 2003, refits the calibration, bakes today’s regime, then runs the sanity check. If the check fails, the bake does not publish.

Market prices move every day; payrolls arrive monthly; GDP and China’s credit stock arrive quarterly and late. A series that has gone quiet past its allowed age stops voting and disappears from the table rather than casting a stale opinion. Methods and today’s fitted centres are on Math.

Regime components

Each component answers one question: is cash cheap or scarce, is the price of money easy or tight, is real activity strong or soft, is price pressure hot or cold, is the market calm or frightened.

  • Chevron — ▲ up, ▼ down, – flat. Which way it has moved over the lookback you picked. This is the turn, not the level.
  • Word — Liquidity Easing / Neutral / Tightening; Rates Easy / Neutral / Tight; Growth Strong / Mid / Soft; Inflation Hot / Mid / Cold; Risk Risk-on / Neutral / Risk-off.
  • Score and needle — −1.5 to +1.5, colour changing at ±0.45. Positive is the reflationary end. If the indicators behind it disagree sharply, the label turns white.

All five share one axis. Green is the reflationary end — more cash, easier funding, firmer growth, hotter prices, more appetite for risk. Red is the contractionary end — the same five things the other way. Amber is mixed or near the middle. Neither colour means good or bad. Green on Inflation is Hot: prices are running, not the app approving. That is the one trap. The same three colours on the six asset classes mean in favor, mixed, and out of favor.

Green — reflationary: easier, stronger, hotter, risk-on Amber — mixed, or near the middle Red — contractionary: tighter, softer, colder, risk-off

1w · 2w · 1m is the lookback for the turn. One month is the default. It drives chevrons, sparklines, asset-class needles, and the narrative. It does not recolour the components. The five are where things are; the lookback is which way they are heading. Inflation can be red and turning up — two different facts.

Dollars are made in more than one place. Liquidity watches G4 balance sheets and the broad dollar, not only the Fed. Rates includes German, UK, and Japanese 10-years. Growth and Inflation are still US reads. China’s credit stock lags two to three quarters, so it votes Liquidity at half weight; the China balance-sheet leg is FX reserves, not the PBoC total.

The six asset classes

Under the five sits a strip of six: Treasuries (5 / 10 / 30), Credit (IG / HY), Equities (Cy / Df), Crypto, Gold, Commodity (Oi / Cu). Green, amber, and red mean in, mixed, and out of favor. The needle shows lean within the call. It cannot flip the call.

Credit risk

  • Duration risk rising and credit risk falling → stocks over long Treasuries
  • Duration risk falling and credit risk rising → long Treasuries over stocks
  • Both rising, cash paid → cash over stocks and bonds
  • Both falling → risk assets and duration can both work
  • Treasuries — 5 is the front end (Rates). 10s and 30s are duration. The parent call is the long end only.
  • Credit — IG is duration plus credit. HY is growth and fear, and whether spreads pay you.
  • Equities — fear, growth, and whether you are paid. Cy leans growth; Df leans fear.
  • Crypto — Bitcoin. Easy plumbing into cheap fear. Does not vote a component.
  • Gold — crisis plumbing, an inflation wage, or deflation fear. Sometimes none of those is live, and the app says so.
  • Commodity — copper is industrial growth; oil carries the dollar and supply. They are not the same trade.

Tap a class for why, the split, and what history says. Full regime inside that card is the longer sheet, including Watch — the print that would prove the call wrong.

What happened last time

The app finds the days in its 23-year record whose five-component mix most resembles today, and reports what happened next: the median move and how often it went that way. A base rate, not a forecast.

A close match is worth weight; a distant match is context. When history disagrees with today’s call, the class title on the strip turns white.

How we know it is right

Nothing here has ever broken loudly. Every real bug ran clean, produced a plausible number, and stated something false with total confidence. Four standing checks, plus a forward record. The daily bake will not publish if the first check fails.

  • Sanity — recomputes all eleven boxes independently and confirms they match what was published.
  • Band audit — flags a series pinned at an extreme, or a colour that almost never fires.
  • Episode audit — lines the five up against 24 named events, each with an expected reading written from what actually happened.
  • Call audit — whether classes called in favor actually outperformed the ones called out, on independent windows, not one episode counted fifty times.

The distinction the five rest on: Rates reads Easy through the 2013 taper tantrum. The 10-year went from 1.6% to 3.0% in four months — that looks wrong until you notice the 2-year sat at 0.2%, QE was still running, and the curve steepened. Rates were easy the whole way; they became less easy. The drama lives in the turn. A reading that went red there would be reporting the turn and calling it the level.

The only test that cannot flatter the app is forward time. Each morning the six calls are written down and later marked. That record began in September 2026. It prints empty on purpose until a class has twelve independent windows.

Honest limits

  • The archive starts in 2003. It has never seen the 1970s, and it has never seen Volcker.
  • Some economic vintages only reach back to around 2016; before that the app is reading revised figures.
  • Bitcoin’s record starts in 2014. Its base rates are not an equal sample beside Treasuries or equities.
  • “In favor” means this mix has historically rewarded owning this, over about a one-month horizon. A base rate, not a target, and not advice.
  • Bands and checklists were fitted on 2003–2026 and graded on 2003–2026. The exact weights are judgment against maybe five independent macro cycles. That is a real overfitting risk. The forward record is the protection.
